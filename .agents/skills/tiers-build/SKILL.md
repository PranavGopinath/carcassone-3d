# Tiers Build Skill (Prompt 1)

This skill guides the **Lead Architect** and specialized subagents through the one-shot build of **Tiers**: a deterministic, Carcassonne-style tile game where the landscape has elevation levels. It is a single-page Three.js + TypeScript + Vite app. All assets are procedural (no external images, fonts, audio files, GLTFs, or CDNs; the game itself needs no API keys).

Target: a 3-minute hackathon demo with 2-player local pass-and-play on one screen, and an animated 2D-to-3D transition as the visual showpiece.

---

## 1. The Core Principles

1. **Tiles are edge-first.** Every terrain feature must meet the tile edge at a defined socket so tiles connect edge to edge like Carcassonne. Features drawn in corners or floating in the middle of a tile are bugs. Tile art is generated by code from tile data and verified with pixel probes. Never ask an image model to draw tiles.
2. **Elevation lives on edges & grass hills.** Each edge has its own level offset. Elevation changes occur via:
   - **Waterfalls:** River plunges from high edge (level 0) down to low edge (level -1).
   - **Grass Hills:** Natural green hill slopes and stepped terraces raise elevation from level 0 up to plateau level +1.
   - **Hill-Bridges:** Roads climb arches from level 0 to level +1 over rivers.
   This lets the world organically branch downwards (sunken river canyons) and upwards (terraced plateaus).
3. **The Start Tile (`START_WATERFALL_BRIDGE`):** The first river tile is a waterfall dropping to lower elevation, spanned by an arch bridge overhead. Players can branch immediately into either **water below/above** or **road across the bridge**.
4. **Multiple Branching & 3D Top-Point Framing:** The landscape supports multiple branching paths (river forks, road hubs, tiered cliffs). In the 3D isometric view, the diorama frames the highest elevated tile/plateau as the visual crown at the top, cascading down to the lower river terraces.
5. **Build for inspection.** A separate feedback loop audits your output with deterministic tests and multimodal review. Expose the hooks in the "Inspector API" section exactly as specified on `window.__game`.
6. **Do not polish.** The first build will not be pretty. Pass the gates, expose the hooks, then stop. Polish is the loop's job.

## 2. 2D Carcassonne Reference & Socket Contracts

Inspect [`reference/pic162877.jpg`](file:///Users/elilimarakathalingasivam/Documents/GitHub/carcassone-3d/reference/pic162877.jpg) for authentic 2D Carcassonne tile layouts:
- **Two-edge features:**
  - **Corner Cities:** City covering two adjacent edges (e.g. North and East), wrapping the corner with continuous wall crenellations, blue-slate roofs, and windows.
  - **Straight City Corridors:** Dual city sections on opposing edges (e.g. North and South) separated by an open grass/river channel.
  - **River Bends & Roads:** Rivers and roads covering 2 adjacent or 2 opposing edges.
  - **Special 2D Features:** Cloisters/Monasteries in meadows, river springs flowing out of rocky hills, and arched road-over-river crossings.

### Socket Geometry Rules

Each tile is a square with edges ordered `[N, E, S, W]`. Each edge has exactly ONE type: `GRASS | ROAD | CITY | WATER`.

| Edge Type | Edge Specification |
|---|---|
| **ROAD** | A path 16% of tile width, centered on the edge midpoint. Away from the midpoint along the edge (t < 0.25 or t > 0.75) it is grass. |
| **WATER** | A river 20% of tile width, centered on the edge midpoint. Same grass boundary rule. |
| **CITY** | Fills the whole edge: city covers >=90% of edge length, depth >=28% of tile width at midpoint, tapering toward corners along curved inner boundary with darker wall rim. |
| **GRASS** | Grass along the entire edge (can represent flat meadow or sloping hill terrace). |

- **Corners are GRASS** unless two adjacent edges are both CITY, in which case the city fill wraps the shared corner.
- Roads and rivers never touch corners; they run from edge midpoints to tile center.
- Features cannot exist unless they reach an edge socket, with 3 exceptions: end-caps at center joined to their single edge (spring, lake, city gate), cloisters, or junction markers.

### Procedural Painter (`src/tiles/painter.ts`)
Exports `paintTile(ctx, spec, rotation, size = 256)` accepting any `CanvasRenderingContext2D` (runs in browser and `@napi-rs/canvas` in Node). Drawing order:
1. Seamless grass base with subtle noise and mowing stripes; for hill tiles, draw contoured elevation rings or terraced shading.
2. Cities (edge band, corner merge, 3-edge block with grass notch), wall rim, blue-slate roof rows, small windows.
3. Rivers and roads as strokes from midpoint to center: straight if opposite edges, quadratic curve through center if adjacent. Constant width, round joins, darker bank line for rivers, cobble speckle for roads.
4. Specials:
   - `START_WATERFALL_BRIDGE`: Vertical cliff line at center with foam spray, plunge pool below, and an arched stone bridge crossing over.
   - `SPRING`: Bubbling mountain pool with rocky contours.
   - `LAKE`: Ellipse basin terminating river.
   - `WATERFALL`: Cliff line with foam band and mist gradient.
   - `BRIDGE_FLAT` & `BRIDGE_HILL`: Arched stone decks, parapets, and slope chevrons.
   - `CLOISTER`: Red-roofed monastery building centered in meadow.
5. Level cue: thin colored tab at each edge differing from level 0 (blue = lower, amber = higher).
6. Tile border: 2px darker rounded border and soft inner shadow.

### Socket Probes (`src/tiles/probe.ts`) & Vitest Tests
In `tests/tiles/sockets.test.ts`, render every unique tile at 4 rotations, classifying pixels by nearest palette class (`PALETTE`):
- ROAD/WATER: Midpoint is feature class; points at t = 0.1 and 0.9 (inset 3px) are GRASS.
- CITY: Points at t = 0.15, 0.5, 0.85 (inset 3px) are CITY.
- GRASS: Points at t = 0.1, 0.5, 0.9 are GRASS.
- Corners (inset 4px) are GRASS unless both adjacent edges are CITY.
- **Stitch test:** For every legal edge-to-edge pairing, the 8 pixels along the shared seam match across tiles within small tolerance.
- **Rotation test:** Rotating a spec by r*90 degrees produces edges and probe results matching the rotated edge array.

## 3. Elevation Model & Branching Architecture

- Tiles carry `edgeLevels: [N, E, S, W]` (integers, tile-local offsets). Flat tiles have `[0, 0, 0, 0]`.
- Placed tiles have a world `level` (integer). Initial tile starts at level 0.
- **Placement legality:**
  1. Cell is empty and touches >= 1 placed tile by edge.
  2. Every shared edge has matching feature types.
  3. **Level consistency:** For each placed neighbor on side `s`, the implied level is:
     `neighbor.level + neighbor.edgeLevels[opposite(s)] - edgeLevels[s]`.
     All implied levels must agree, becoming the new tile's `level`.
- **Level Changers:**
  - **`START_WATERFALL_BRIDGE`:** North water high (0), South water low (-1), East/West road flat (0).
  - **`WATERFALL_STRAIGHT` / `WATERFALL_BEND`:** Water drops 1 level (high = 0, low = -1). Water flows high to low.
  - **`GRASS_HILL`:** Pure grass tiles that slope or terrace upward, raising one or more edges to level +1.
  - **`BRIDGE_HILL`:** Road climbs S(0) up to N(+1) over level-0 river.
- **Multiple Branching Points:**
  - Players can branch out into multiple directions from 3-way/4-way junctions (`ROAD_BRANCH_3WAY`, `RIVER_FORK_DELTA`, `CITY_ROAD_HUB`).
  - Elevation branching splits the board into sunken river canyons (level -1), the main fertile valley (level 0), and raised hill plateaus (level +1).

## 5. Players, Turn Flow, and Scoring

- 2 players (Player 1: Deep Blue, Player 2: Terracotta/Amber), 3 meeples each.
- **Turn sequence:** View drawn tile in dock -> Rotate (`R` or button) -> Click glowing legal ghost spot (shows implied level badge `L-1`, `L0`, `L+1`) -> Optionally place 1 meeple on City, Road, or River feature -> Turn passes with banner.
- A meeple cannot be placed on a feature whose connected chain already holds a meeple.
- **Immediate Elevation Change Bonus:**
  - Whenever a placed tile changes elevation (e.g., waterfall drop to `L-1`, grass hill rise to `L+1`, or hill-bridge climb to `L+1`), the active player immediately scores **+2 points**!
- **Completion Rules:**
  - **City:** Complete when no open CITY edges remain.
  - **Road:** Complete when both ends terminate (hut, junction, or city gate) or forms a closed loop.
  - **River:** Complete when both ends are capped by SPRING or LAKE (or forms a loop).
  - Completed chains score immediately and return meeples to players' reserves.
- **Completed Chain Scoring:**
  - **ROAD:** **+1 point** for every connected tile in the completed road (+3 bonus per bridge tile in the chain).
  - **CITY:** **+2 points** per tile in the completed city.
  - **RIVER (WATER):** **+2 points** per tile in the completed river (+3 bonus per waterfall tile in the chain).
  - **Ownership:** The player with the most meeples on the chain takes all points; on a tie, all tied players get full points.
- **End-Game Incomplete Scoring (after 20th tile):**
  - Any feature with a meeple that remains unconnected / incomplete at the end scores **half the points they would have been**:
    - **Incomplete City:** **1 pt per tile** (half of 2 pts/tile).
    - **Incomplete River:** **1 pt per tile** (half of 2 pts/tile).
    - **Incomplete Road:** **0.5 pts per tile** (half of 1 pt/tile, calculated as `Math.floor(tiles / 2)` or 0.5 per tile).
    - Grass never scores (no farmers).

## 6. 3D Terraced Representation & Camera Framing

- `LEVEL_HEIGHT = 0.5` (tile side = 1). Edge world height = `(level + edgeLevel) * LEVEL_HEIGHT`.
- Each tile is a subdivided plane (16x16) with per-vertex height from `tileHeight(spec, u, v)`:
  - Normal: flat.
  - `GRASS_HILL`: Smooth ramp / contoured terrace rising toward the high edge(s).
  - `START_WATERFALL_BRIDGE`: Flat high half, plunge drop, carved canyon floor, with an elevated arched bridge mesh spanning across the chasm.
  - `WATERFALL`: Cliff face with falling water sheet, crest/base foam, and mist particles.
  - `BRIDGE_HILL`: Road ramp up to arch deck with river flowing underneath.
- **Cliff Skirts & Triplanar Shading:** Generated along height boundaries and outer board perimeter, textured with strata rock layers that never stretch.
- **Camera & 3D Top-Point Framing:**
  - Single `THREE.PerspectiveCamera`. Never swapped.
  - **2D View:** Top-down on Y, FOV 8 degrees (flat orthographic projection, height scale = 0).
  - **3D View:** 35-degree isometric tilt, FOV 45 degrees.
  - **Top-Point Crown Framing:** The 3D view camera target is dynamically centered on the board's center of mass while orienting the pitch/yaw so that the highest elevation plateau tiles crown the top of the scene, creating a rich multi-tiered 3D amphitheater.
  - **1.4s Morph Tween:** Eased transition interpolating camera position, lookAt, FOV, and terrain height-scale (0 to 1).

## 8. Inspector API (Required on `window.__game`)

- **State & Play:** `getState()`, `getLegalMoves()`, `applyMove(move)`, `runReplay(moves)`, `setView('2d'|'3d')`, `setQuality(...)`, `setTheme(...)`, `startDemo()`.
- **Deterministic Inspection Timeline:** Loading with `?inspect=1&seed=7&w=1280&h=720` freezes clocks. `__game.renderAt(tSeconds)` renders exact frames:
  - `0-3s`: Top-down flat view.
  - `3-5s`: The Rise (1.4s morph + settle).
  - `5-9s`: 360-degree orbit at 35 degrees.
  - `9-12s`: Macro zoom on waterfall then bridge.
- **Named Camera Shots:** `__game.listShots()` and `__game.captureShot(name)`. Required shots:
  `board-2d`, `terrace-overview`, `waterfall-macro`, `bridge-hill-macro`, `cliff-closeup`, `city-closeup`, `meeple-ground`, `underside-slab`.
- **Tile Sheet:** `__game.getTileSheet()` returns canvas of every unique tile at 4 rotations in a labeled grid.
- **Fixtures:** `__game.loadFixture(name)` (supports `fixture-all-features` containing waterfall drop, hill bridge, grass hill plateau, city corner, and meeples).
- **Geometry Audit:** `__game.audit()` returns JSON report verifying:
  - No floating props (gap > epsilon).
  - Overlays have `polygonOffset` or unique y-offset.
  - No UV stretch outliers on cliff triangles.
  - Meeples grounded on terrain height.
  - Water surface heights match at shared edges.
  - Zero WebGL/console warnings.
